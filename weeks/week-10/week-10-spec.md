## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **Spain declared war on Britain in 1779 primarily as:**
   a) a direct military ally of the United States, formally recognizing American independence
   b) France's ally, hoping to weaken British power and recover its own territorial losses *(correct)*
   c) a neutral party enforcing a trade agreement
   d) a client state acting on Britain's orders

2. **Bernardo de Galvez's decision to press ahead with his campaign despite the August 1779 hurricane shows that:**
   a) the campaign's success was guaranteed regardless of the storm's damage
   b) Spain's king had ordered him to proceed no matter the cost
   c) a leader's own willingness to accept risk could meaningfully shape whether an event happened at all *(correct)*
   d) hurricanes had no real effect on 18th-century military campaigns

3. **Fort Panmure at Natchez came under Spanish control in 1779 because:**
   a) it was included as a term of the Baton Rouge capitulation, without being directly attacked *(correct)*
   b) its garrison surrendered independently before Galvez's forces arrived
   c) Native nations allied with Spain seized it on their own
   d) Britain voluntarily abandoned the fort months earlier

4. **The 1781 Natchez uprising was led mainly by:**
   a) enslaved people resisting both British and Spanish authority
   b) Choctaw and Chickasaw nations objecting to new land surveys
   c) British loyalist settlers hoping British forces would still relieve the district *(correct)*
   d) French Catholic settlers demanding stricter religious enforcement

5. **Spain's decision not to punish the Natchez District broadly after the 1781 uprising suggests that:**
   a) Spanish officials judged the district's overall stability more valuable than harsh retaliation *(correct)*
   b) Spain lacked the military strength to punish anyone
   c) the uprising had never actually threatened Spanish control
   d) Spain planned to abandon the district shortly afterward

6. **Spanish officials generally handled the district's religious and legal diversity by:**
   a) rigidly enforcing every official policy exactly as written
   b) tolerating private Protestant worship and honoring existing land claims in practice *(correct)*
   c) requiring immediate conversion to Catholicism for all residents
   d) voiding every claim made under earlier French or British rule

7. **This week's combined evidence, the hurricane, the capitulation terms, and the 1781 uprising, best supports the idea that:**
   a) Mississippi's colonial outcomes in this period were fixed in advance regardless of individual decisions
   b) European rivalries in this period had no real effect on Mississippi
   c) specific, changeable circumstances and individual choices shaped what actually happened here, not inevitable destiny *(correct)*
   d) Native nations played no role in this period's outcomes

## Primary Source Exercise 10.1 — Articles of Capitulation, Baton Rouge, 1779

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why would Galvez insist on including Fort Panmure at Natchez in the surrender terms, rather than negotiating only for what his forces had actually besieged at Baton Rouge?
2. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Dickson had refused to include Fort Panmure in the capitulation terms. What might Galvez's Gulf Coast campaign have looked like afterward?
3. **(Conceptual)** This document grants the defeated British garrison the "honors of war" rather than treating them as a disgraced force. What idea about how enemies should treat each other, even in defeat, does that custom reflect?
4. **(Conceptual)** This page describes the capitulation's content rather than quoting a verified transcription of its exact wording. Why does that distinction matter when you evaluate how confidently you can rely on a historical account?

## Primary Source Exercise 10.2 — Natchez District Land and Court Records, 1767–1805

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Why would a settler's land claim being documented across three different governments and three different languages actually strengthen, rather than weaken, that settler's legal position?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why did the United States, upon acquiring the Natchez District, choose to honor existing land claims rather than requiring every settler to reapply under entirely new American rules?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose each government that controlled the Natchez District had voided all land claims made under the previous government. How might that have affected settlement and stability in the district over time?
4. **(Conceptual)** This page describes the land-claim collection's scope rather than quoting one specific verified document. What does that distinction suggest about the difference between a source being real and well-documented versus a source being directly quotable?

## Discussion

**Prompt:** This week's lessons argued that Fort Panmure at Natchez changed hands twice, once through capitulation terms in 1779 and once (briefly) through the 1781 uprising, without either change reflecting what the district's actual residents wanted. Do you think ordinary settlers in a period like this had any real influence over their own political future, or was it almost entirely decided by distant negotiators and commanders? Use specific details from this week's lessons and primary sources to support your position. Respond to a classmate who reached a different conclusion than you did.

**What you'll need:** VR headset (desktop/mobile fallback available), this week's two lessons and Primary Source 10.1 already read.
