📖 BACKGROUND

On September 21, 1779, following a Spanish artillery bombardment that badly damaged the fort's defenses, British Lieutenant Colonel Alexander Dickson agreed to surrender Baton Rouge to Spanish governor Bernardo de Galvez. The two commanders then negotiated formal Articles of Capitulation, a legal document spelling out exactly what the surrender covered and how it would be carried out. The original document, and a later 1815 copy of its first seven articles made by a court clerk named A. Winthrop, are held today by the Tulane University Digital Library in New Orleans.

This page describes the document's content rather than quoting a specific numbered article directly, since a verified line-by-line transcription of the articles could not be confirmed in this pass. What is well established across multiple historical accounts of the battle is this: the terms Dickson agreed to did not stop at Baton Rouge. They required the British to also surrender Fort Panmure, the post at Natchez more than a hundred miles upriver, along with its entire garrison, even though Galvez's forces had never marched on Natchez or exchanged a single shot with the troops stationed there.

📜 WHAT THE DOCUMENT ESTABLISHES

The capitulation formally declared the British garrison at Baton Rouge prisoners of war, but, in recognition of what military accounts describe as a genuinely determined defense of the fort, Galvez granted the defeated garrison the honors of war, a customary gesture allowing defeated soldiers to march out with certain ceremonial dignities intact rather than being treated as a disgraced or broken force. The document's inclusion of Fort Panmure at Natchez as a separate condition of surrender is the detail this course returns to directly: an entire post and its garrison changed allegiance purely through the terms two commanders wrote down at a table outside Baton Rouge, not through any engagement fought at Natchez itself.

Document description drawn from the item record and secondary battle accounts; the original and the 1815 clerk's copy are held by the Tulane University Digital Library.
