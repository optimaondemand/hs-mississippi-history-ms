Standard: 3.1. Virtue: Courage (Q1).

## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **De Soto's expedition was primarily motivated by:**
   a) a desire to establish permanent farming colonies
   b) hope of finding another wealthy empire like the Inca or Aztec *(correct)*
   c) a mission to convert Native nations to Christianity above all else
   d) a scientific survey of North American geography

2. **The Battle of Mabila in Alabama mattered for Mississippi's history because:**
   a) it left De Soto's expedition smaller and more depleted before it reached Mississippi *(correct)*
   b) it convinced De Soto to turn back to Spain immediately
   c) it happened after the expedition had already left Mississippi
   d) it had no lasting effect on the expedition at all

3. **De Soto's expedition differed from later French, English, and American settlement mainly because it:**
   a) never made contact with any Native nation
   b) lasted more than two centuries
   c) built no permanent towns or lasting government *(correct)*
   d) was welcomed everywhere it went without conflict

4. **The Chickasaw attacked De Soto's camp mainly because:**
   a) De Soto refused to share any food with them
   b) De Soto announced he was leaving Chickasaw territory
   c) the Chickasaw wanted De Soto's horses for religious ceremonies
   d) De Soto demanded Chickasaw men serve as forced porters *(correct)*

5. **The Chicaza attack's most immediate consequence for De Soto's expedition was:**
   a) the total surrender of the expedition
   b) a permanent alliance with the Chickasaw
   c) the loss of significant equipment, supplies, and horses *(correct)*
   d) the conversion of the Chickasaw to Christianity

6. **De Soto's expedition is now understood to have contributed to Native population decline mainly through:**
   a) direct warfare with every nation it encountered
   b) a deliberate campaign of biological warfare
   c) forced relocation of entire nations
   d) the unintentional spread of European disease *(correct)*

7. **The roughly one century between De Soto's expedition and the next serious European colonization attempt shows that:**
   a) Spanish control of Mississippi was continuous the entire time
   b) European colonization of Mississippi was not a single continuous process *(correct)*
   c) no European power was ever interested in the region again
   d) De Soto successfully established a lasting colony

## Primary Source Exercise 6.1 — A Gentleman of Elvas, Narrative of the De Soto Expedition

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** What does the specific detail of "six hundred men" and "seven ships" suggest about how seriously the Spanish crown and de Soto himself took this venture?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why would an expedition of this size need such careful advance preparation, compared to a smaller group of explorers?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose de Soto had organized a much smaller expedition of only a few dozen men. How might that have changed his ability to demand food, labor, and safe passage from Native towns along the way?
4. **(Conceptual)** This account was written by a member of the expedition itself. What kinds of things might such an author be inclined to leave out or minimize about the expedition's conduct toward Native peoples?

---

## Primary Source Exercise 6.2 — The Attack at Chicaza

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why would a demand for forced labor specifically, rather than a demand for food or goods, be likely to provoke such a severe response from the Chickasaw?
2. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the Chickasaw had launched a second attack immediately after the first, while the Spanish were still recovering. Based on what you know about the expedition's condition, what might have happened?
3. **(Conceptual)** This lesson describes the Chicaza attack using historical background rather than a word-for-word quotation from the original account. What does the difference between description and direct quotation mean for how confidently you should treat specific details?
4. **(Conceptual)** Why might it matter, when studying this event, to consider it from the Chickasaw's perspective as well as from the perspective of the Spanish chronicler who is the primary surviving source?

**What you're doing:** You'll trace De Soto's route into and through Mississippi on a large map, marking key events, then reflect on the gap between the expedition's goals and what it actually achieved.

**What you'll need:** VR headset (desktop/mobile fallback available), this week's two lessons and two primary sources already read.

**How to join:** [Session type: temporary] · Session ID: [TBD] · Location: [TBD — a Location with a large regional map surface; specific Location to be confirmed once available in the ENGAGE library] · Spawn point: at the map table.

**What to do, in order:**
1. Using pins or a Whiteboard IFX, mark the expedition's approximate route from Florida through Alabama into Mississippi, noting the Battle of Mabila and the Chicaza winter camp.
2. Place a sticky note at the Chicaza camp location summarizing what triggered the attack there.
3. Place a second sticky note noting what the expedition ultimately failed to achieve, despite everything it cost.

**What to submit:** Your spatial recording, submitted through Canvas.

**Facilitator note:** Students may want to treat this expedition purely as an adventure story. Redirect toward the analytical question: intention versus consequence.
