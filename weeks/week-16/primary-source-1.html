📖 BACKGROUND

The Revised Code of the Statute Laws of the State of Mississippi, adopted in 1857, compiled the state's laws, including its detailed slave code, into a single official volume. The complete original is digitized and held by HathiTrust Digital Library. The excerpts below are drawn from a documented transcription of the code's articles on slave offenses and conduct.

As you read, notice how specific and detailed the code's restrictions are, covering everything from travel to assembly to physical punishment, and think about what that level of specificity suggests about how seriously the state took the task of controlling enslaved people's daily lives.

📜 FROM THE CODE
"No slave shall go from the tenement of his master or employer… without a pass, or some letter or token whereby it may appear that he is proceeding by proper authority." (Article 45)
"All meetings or assemblies of slaves… above the number of five… at any place of public resort… shall be deemed an unlawful assembly." (Article 51)
"Any slave who shall commit an assault and battery on a white person… shall be punished by any number of lashes on the bare back, not exceeding one hundred." (Article 54)
"If any slave shall be guilty of the murder of any human being… or shall commit a rape… shall… suffer death." (Article 58)

Full code held by HathiTrust Digital Library. Excerpts above drawn from a documented transcription of the code's articles on slave offenses.
