## Primary Source Exercise 16.1 — Revised Code of the Statute Laws of Mississippi, 1857

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Article 45's pass requirement placed control over an enslaved person's movement entirely in someone else's hands. What does that requirement reveal about how the law understood an enslaved person's basic freedom of movement?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why might Mississippi's lawmakers have specifically restricted assemblies of more than five enslaved people, rather than leaving gatherings unregulated?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the 1857 code had included no punishment provisions at all, leaving enforcement entirely to individual owners' discretion. How might that have changed how the code actually functioned in practice?
4. **(Conceptual)** This code assigns specific, escalating punishments to specific offenses. What does that structure suggest about how thoroughly the law's authors had thought through controlling nearly every aspect of enslaved people's conduct?

## Primary Source Exercise 16.2 — James Lucas, WPA Slave Narrative

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, conceptual, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why might a formerly enslaved person compare sharecropping directly to slavery, rather than describing it as something entirely new and different?
2. **(Conceptual)** This page quotes only one confirmed sentence from Lucas's interview rather than a longer, unverified account. Why does that restraint matter when the subject matter is this serious?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose sharecropping had actually given formerly enslaved workers full ownership of the land they farmed. How might Lucas's assessment of it likely have been different?
4. **(Conceptual)** Lucas was over one hundred years old when interviewed in the 1930s. What does the very long span between the events he described and the interview itself suggest about both the value and the limits of his testimony?
