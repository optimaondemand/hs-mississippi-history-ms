📖 BACKGROUND

James Lucas, born into slavery in 1833 in Adams County, Mississippi, was interviewed as part of the WPA Federal Writers' Project's Slave Narrative Project in the 1930s, when he was over one hundred years old. His interview is preserved as part of the Library of Congress's Born in Slavery collection. Adams County, home to Natchez, was one of Mississippi's oldest and most heavily enslaved counties, a place this course has returned to repeatedly across earlier weeks.

The excerpt below reflects Lucas's own assessment of sharecropping, the labor system that replaced slavery after emancipation, drawn to your attention because of how directly it connects slavery's legacy to the economic system that followed it, a connection this course's later units on Reconstruction will examine in far greater depth.

📜 FROM THE NARRATIVE
"[Sharecropping] wuzn't much diffent from slavery."

This excerpt is drawn from a documented secondary account of Lucas's interview. The full interview transcript is preserved by the Library of Congress as part of Born in Slavery: Slave Narratives from the Federal Writers' Project, 1936–1938. Only this single sentence could be independently confirmed in this pass; the complete interview was not directly accessible here.

💡 A NOTE ON THIS SOURCE
This page quotes only what could be independently confirmed, rather than presenting a longer, unverified account of Lucas's full interview as though it had been directly checked. Explore the complete collection, including Lucas's full interview and hundreds of other Mississippi narratives, at the Library of Congress.
