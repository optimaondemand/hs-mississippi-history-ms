The Treaty of Hopewell, 1786

📖 BACKGROUND

By 1786, the American Revolution had ended and the newly independent United States needed to establish its own relationships with Native nations that had previously dealt mainly with Britain, France, and Spain. The Treaty of Hopewell, signed at Hopewell on the Keowee River, established one of the first formal agreements between the United States and the Choctaw Nation.

As you read, consider what this treaty suggests about how the new United States government wanted to be seen by Native nations, and what the Choctaw negotiators may have hoped to gain by signing it.

📜 FROM THE TREATY
"The Commissioners Plenipotentiary of all the Choctaw nation, shall restore all the prisoners, citizens of the United States, or subjects of their allies, to their entire liberty, if any there be in the Choctaw nation."
This is Article I. The treaty's remaining ten articles go on to cover several other major topics, including:
  • A description of the boundary lines of Choctaw territory as recognized by the United States at that time.
  • A statement that the United States, not any state government or private individual, held sole authority to manage trade and treaty relations with the Choctaw Nation.
