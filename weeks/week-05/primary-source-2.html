📖 BACKGROUND

On May 22, 1945, the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians adopted a written constitution under the federal Indian Reorganization Act, formally organizing the modern tribal government that still governs the nation today. Unlike the treaties you've read so far this week, which were negotiated between a Native nation and an outside government, this document was written by the Choctaw people themselves, for their own government.

The constitution's articles cover the tribe's name and jurisdiction, membership, its governing body and elections, the powers and duties of the Tribal Council and Chief, and the rights of tribal members, along with a set of bylaws. As you explore it, think about what it means for a nation to write its own founding document nearly two centuries after most of that nation had already been forced out of the state.

💡 READ THE CONSTITUTION
Read the full constitution at the tribe's own official site: choctaw.org. It is also archived by the National Indian Law Library.
This page describes the document's structure and significance rather than quoting its specific article text, since the authoritative version is a formatted legal document best read directly at the source above.
