📖 BACKGROUND

On March 1, 1817, the Fourteenth Congress passed "An Act to enable the people of the western part of the Mississippi territory to form a constitution and state government, and for the admission of such state into the union, on an equal footing with the original states." The full text is preserved in the official U.S. Statutes at Large (3 Stat. 348) and hosted today by the Library of Congress's Century of Lawmaking collection via govinfo.gov, the federal government's own authenticated document repository.

As you read the excerpts below, compare the boundaries described here to the much larger territory described in Primary Source 12.1, and notice how many conditions Congress attached before this new state could actually be admitted.

📜 FROM THE ACT
"That the inhabitants of the western part of the Mississippi territory be, and they hereby are, authorized to form for themselves a constitution and state government, and to assume such name as they shall deem proper; and the said state, when formed, shall be admitted into the union upon the same footing with the original states, in all respects whatever." (Section 1)
"Beginning on the river Mississippi at the point where the southern boundary line of the state of Tennessee strikes the same, thence east along the said boundary line to the Tennessee river… thence due south to the Gulf of Mexico… thence up said river to the thirty-first degree of north latitude, thence west along the said degree of latitude to the Mississippi river, thence up the same to the beginning." (Section 2)
"Which convention, when met, shall first determine, by a majority of the whole number elected, whether it be or be not expedient, at that time, to form a constitution and state government for the people within the said territory… Provided, That the same, when formed, shall be republican, and not repugnant to the principles of the ordinance of the thirteenth of July, one thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven…" (Section 4)
"The river Mississippi, and the navigable rivers and waters leading into the same, or into the Gulf of Mexico, shall be common highways, and for ever free, as well to the inhabitants of the said state, as to other citizens of the United States, without any tax, duty, impost, or toll, therefor, imposed by the said state." (Section 4)

Full act text hosted by the Library of Congress Century of Lawmaking collection via govinfo.gov; official citation 3 Stat. 348 (Fourteenth Congress, Sess. II, Ch. 23).
