# Week 12 Spec — The Road to Statehood: Mississippi Territory Becomes a State

Standard: 4.3. Virtue: Self-government (Q2).

---

## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **The 1798 Mississippi Territory Act's first sections address:**
   a) a boundary dispute between the United States and the state of Georgia *(correct)*
   b) a trade agreement with Spain over the Mississippi River
   c) a treaty renegotiation with the Choctaw Nation
   d) a religious dispute among the territory's settlers

2. **The 1798 act's government structure was modeled on:**
   a) the government of the state of Georgia
   b) the Northwest Ordinance's system for the territory northwest of the Ohio River *(correct)*
   c) an entirely new system invented specifically for Mississippi
   d) the government of Spanish Louisiana

3. **Regarding slavery, the 1798 Territory Act:**
   a) banned slavery entirely, exactly as the Northwest Ordinance had
   b) left the subject completely unaddressed
   c) deliberately excluded the Northwest Ordinance's anti-slavery article while restricting direct importation from outside the United States *(correct)*
   d) required immediate emancipation within the territory

4. **The territorial governor's combined role as superintendent of Indian affairs for the southern department shows that:**
   a) treaty relations with Native nations were handled entirely separately from territorial governance
   b) the governor had no authority over Indian affairs at all
   c) the territory's governance and its relations with Native nations ran through the same office *(correct)*
   d) only the federal government in Washington handled treaty negotiations directly

5. **The 1817 Enabling Act authorized statehood for:**
   a) the entire original 1798 Mississippi Territory, unchanged
   b) only the counties directly bordering the Gulf Coast
   c) a combined Mississippi-Alabama single state
   d) only the western part of the territory, with the eastern part becoming Alabama Territory *(correct)*

6. **The county-by-county representative apportionment in the 1817 act reveals that:**
   a) representation was weighted toward more heavily settled counties like Adams *(correct)*
   b) every county received an identical number of representatives regardless of population
   c) representatives were assigned randomly by lottery
   d) only Natchez itself was permitted to send representatives

7. **The 1817 act's conditions on statehood, including the "expedient" vote requirement and federal land protections, best support the idea that:**
   a) Mississippi's self-government was entirely unconditional
   b) genuine self-government and real external constraint coexisted in the same admission process *(correct)*
   c) Congress had no real authority over how the new state's constitution was written
   d) the enabling act guaranteed immediate statehood regardless of the convention's own decision

## Primary Source Exercise 12.1 — Mississippi Territory Act, 1798

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** The act specifically names "the last article" of the Northwest Ordinance as excluded, rather than simply not mentioning slavery at all. What does that specificity suggest about how deliberately this choice was made?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why might Congress have chosen to restrict the direct importation of enslaved people from outside the United States while still permitting slavery generally within the new territory?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the 1798 act had included the Northwest Ordinance's anti-slavery article rather than excluding it. How might Mississippi's economic and social development have looked different in the decades that followed?
4. **(Conceptual)** This act combines the roles of territorial governor and superintendent of Indian affairs into one office. What does that combination suggest about how the federal government viewed the relationship between governing settlers and managing relations with Native nations?

---

## Primary Source Exercise 12.2 — Mississippi Enabling Act, 1817

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, conceptual, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why might Congress have required the convention to explicitly vote on whether statehood was "expedient" rather than simply proceeding automatically once the enabling act passed?
2. **(Conceptual)** The act requires the new state to keep the Mississippi River "common highways, and for ever free" to all U.S. citizens, not just Mississippians. What does this condition suggest about how Congress balanced the new state's own interests against the interests of the wider nation?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Congress had admitted the entire original 1798 territory as one single state instead of splitting it into Mississippi and Alabama. How might that have changed the political character of the resulting state?
4. **(Conceptual)** The act ties Mississippi's road and canal funding to debts owed to Georgia being paid off first. What does that sequencing reveal about how connected Mississippi's own statehood was to unresolved obligations from Week 12's Lesson 12.1?

## Discussion

**Prompt:** This week's lessons showed that Mississippi's path to statehood combined genuine self-government (the territory's own people writing their own constitution) with real external constraints (federal conditions on land, rivers, and the form of government itself). Do you think this counts as authentic self-government, or is a constitution written under this many conditions something less than full self-rule? Use specific details from this week's lessons and primary sources to support your position. Respond to a classmate who reached a different conclusion than you did.
