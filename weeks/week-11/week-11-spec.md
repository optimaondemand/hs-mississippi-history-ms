## Primary Source Exercise 11.1 — Treaty of Doak's Stand, 1820

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** The treaty's preamble says its purpose is to "perpetuate" the Choctaw Nation. What does that word choice suggest about how the United States government wanted this treaty to be understood, regardless of what happened later?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why might the United States have wanted to fund Choctaw schools as part of this treaty rather than simply negotiating a land exchange alone?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Mississippi's legislature had never extended state law over the Choctaw Nation in the years after this treaty. Do you think a treaty as total as Dancing Rabbit Creek would still have happened in 1830? Why or why not?
4. **(Conceptual)** Article IX allowed Choctaw families inside the ceded land to keep a full square mile if they wished to stay. What does this provision suggest about how negotiated this treaty actually was, compared to a simple order to vacate the land?

---

## Primary Source Exercise 11.2 — Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek, 1830

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, conceptual, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why would the treaty's own preamble specifically mention that the federal government "cannot protect" the Choctaw people from Mississippi's laws, rather than simply stating that removal was federal policy?
2. **(Conceptual)** Article IV promises the Choctaw Nation self-government in its new territory. What does it mean for a government to promise self-government to a people it has just required to leave their own homeland?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose land agents had processed Article XIV claims honestly, with no fraud. How might Mississippi's Choctaw population have looked different in the decades that followed?
4. **(Conceptual)** This treaty keeps one promise (Article IV's self-government guarantee) far more successfully than another (Article XIV's promise to those who wished to stay). What does that gap suggest about judging a government by its written promises alone, versus judging it by what actually happened?
