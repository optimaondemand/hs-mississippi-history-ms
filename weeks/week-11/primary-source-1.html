Treaty of Doak's Stand, 1820
Primary Source
📖 BACKGROUND

On October 18, 1820, United States commissioners Andrew Jackson and Thomas Hinds met with Choctaw Mingoes, chiefs, and warriors at a trading post on the Natchez Road called Doak's Stand. The resulting treaty ceded a portion of Choctaw land in central Mississippi in exchange for a much larger tract west of the Mississippi River. The full text is preserved in the official statutes at large and is hosted today by the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma.

As you read the excerpts below, pay close attention to how the treaty's own preamble describes its purpose, and compare that stated purpose to what you already know happened just ten years later at Dancing Rabbit Creek.

📜 FROM THE TREATY
"Whereas it is an important object with the President of the United States, to promote the civilization of the Choctaw Indians, by the establishment of schools amongst them; and to perpetuate them as a nation by exchanging, for a small part of their land here, a country beyond the Mississippi River… James Monroe, President of the United States of America, by Andrew Jackson… and… Thomas Hinds… commissioners Plenipotentiary of the United States, on the one part, and the Mingoes, Head Men, and Warriors, of the Choctaw nation, in full Council assembled, on the other part, have freely and voluntarily entered into the following articles…"
"For the purpose of aiding and assisting the poor Indians, who wish to remove to the country hereby ceded on the part of the United States, and to enable them to do well and support their families, the Commissioners of the United States engage… to give to each warrior a blanket, kettle, rifle gun, bullet moulds and nippers, and ammunition sufficient for hunting and defense, for one year." (Article V)
"All those who have separate settlements, and fall within the limits of the land ceded by the Choctaw nation to the United States, and who desire to remain where they now reside, shall be secured in a tract or parcel of land one mile square, to include their improvements." (Article IX)

Full treaty text hosted by the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma; official citation 7 Stat. 210.
