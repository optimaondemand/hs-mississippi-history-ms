📖 BACKGROUND

You encountered Le Page du Pratz's History of Louisiana in Week 4 as a source on Natchez society. Du Pratz's book also describes the French colony's own founding and early years, drawing on both his own later experience in the colony and on earlier accounts he consulted while writing.

As you read, compare what du Pratz says about Iberville's role and the colony's slow early growth to what this week's lesson already told you.

📜 FROM THE BOOK
"M. d'Hiberville, Chef d'Escadre, having discovered, in 1698, the mouths of the river St. Louis, and being nominated Governor General of that vast country, carried thither the first colony in 1699."
"The settlement was made on the river Mobile, with all the facility that could be wished; but its progress proved slow: for these first inhabitants had no other advantage above the natives, as to the necessaries of life, but what their own industry, joined to some rude tools, to give the plainest forms to timbers, afforded them."

Excerpts transcribed from the edition hosted by Project Gutenberg. "River St. Louis" refers to the Mississippi River.
