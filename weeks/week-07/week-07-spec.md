## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **La Salle's 1682 ceremony at the mouth of the Mississippi River accomplished:**
   a) a legal claim to the territory with no French settlers actually present *(correct)*
   b) the founding of the first permanent French settlement in the region
   c) a peace treaty with the Chickasaw and Choctaw
   d) the founding of New Orleans

2. **Iberville's 1699 expedition differed from La Salle's earlier claim because it:**
   a) rejected France's claim to the territory entirely
   b) was led by a Spanish, not a French, officer
   c) focused entirely on trade with England
   d) arrived with settlers, soldiers, and supplies intended to establish a lasting colony *(correct)*

3. **French Louisiana's early growth was slow mainly because:**
   a) the colony had too many settlers to support
   b) France refused to send any additional settlers after 1699
   c) unfamiliar conditions and practical hardship made sustaining the colony difficult *(correct)*
   d) Spain destroyed the first French settlement

4. **France turned to enslaved African labor in Louisiana mainly because:**
   a) Native nations demanded it as a condition of trade
   b) French indentured labor proved insufficient to meet the colony's needs *(correct)*
   c) enslaved labor was required by the Code Noir
   d) France had no access to any other source of labor anywhere

5. **The Code Noir's first article, addressing Jewish residents, suggests that the code:**
   a) was concerned only with regulating slavery
   b) was written entirely by religious leaders rather than the French crown
   c) applied only to enslaved people, not free colonists
   d) connected religious and social control to its broader purpose *(correct)*

6. **The Code Noir's influence on Mississippi is best described as:**
   a) continuing to shape later slave codes even after French rule ended *(correct)*
   b) ending completely when French control of the region ended
   c) having no measurable effect on later colonial law
   d) applying only to the city of New Orleans

7. **Comparing French colonization to De Soto's earlier expedition shows that:**
   a) both resulted in permanent European settlements
   b) French colonization involved lasting institutions like permanent towns and government, unlike De Soto's expedition *(correct)*
   c) neither expedition made any lasting impact on the region
   d) France arrived in Mississippi before Spain did

## Primary Source Exercise 7.1 — Le Page du Pratz on the Founding of Louisiana

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Du Pratz notes the first settlers had "no other advantage above the natives" except their own tools and industry. What does this admission suggest about the gap between European technology and its actual usefulness in an unfamiliar environment?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why might the colony's "progress" have "proved slow," according to du Pratz, despite France's clear intention to establish a lasting settlement?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the first French settlers had arrived with significantly more supplies and specialized tools. Based on du Pratz's account, would that alone have guaranteed faster progress? Explain your reasoning.
4. **(Conceptual)** Du Pratz wrote this account years after the events it describes, based partly on other people's records. How should that distance in time and firsthand experience affect how you weigh his account against Le Page du Pratz's own eyewitness descriptions of the Natchez from Week 4?

## Primary Source Exercise 7.2 — The Code Noir (1724)

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Why might French colonial law address religious conformity and the regulation of slavery within the same single legal code, rather than as two entirely separate matters?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why would France want to enforce a decades-old anti-Jewish edict from the home country specifically in a brand-new overseas colony?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the Code Noir had never been written, and each individual French colonist in Louisiana set his own rules for enslaved labor. How might daily life for enslaved people, and legal continuity after French rule ended, have looked different?
4. **(Conceptual)** This code's assumptions influenced later Spanish and American slave codes even after French rule ended. What does that continuity suggest about how much a change in colonial government actually changes daily life for the people living under it?
