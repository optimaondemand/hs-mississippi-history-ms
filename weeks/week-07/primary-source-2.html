📖 BACKGROUND

The Code Noir issued for Louisiana in 1724 opens, before addressing slavery at all, with a provision expelling Jewish residents from the colony, referencing an even older French royal decree from more than a century earlier.

As you read, notice that this is the code's very first article, positioned before any of its provisions regulating slavery. Think about what that placement suggests about how French colonial law connected religious control to its broader vision of social order.

📜 FROM THE CODE
"The Edict of 23 April 1615 of the late King Louis XIII of glorious memory, will be in force in our Province and Colony of Louisiana. This accomplished, we enjoin all of our officers to chase from our isles all the Jews who have established residence there. As with all declared enemies of Christianity, we command them to be gone within three months of the day of issuance of the present law, under penalty of…"

Article I of the Code Noir, English translation as published by 64 Parishes. The code's remaining fifty-four articles, which you can read in full at that link, go on to regulate slavery, marriage, religious instruction, and punishment in detail.
