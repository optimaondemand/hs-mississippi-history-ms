📖 BACKGROUND

Everard Green Baker (1828–1890) was a white planter who owned land in Jefferson, Panola, and Hinds Counties, Mississippi. His personal diary and plantation journal, spanning 1848 to 1876, is held today by the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill's Southern Historical Collection (Collection Number 00041). The finding aid describes entries covering crop management, weather, food and household matters, the Civil War home front, and direct accounts of the people Baker enslaved, and confirms that typed transcriptions of the volumes exist as part of the physical collection.

This page describes the collection honestly rather than quoting a specific diary entry directly. The collection's real existence, dates, and contents are confirmed through UNC's own finding aid and its African American Documentary Resources project, but the typed transcriptions themselves could not be located online and freely viewable in this pass, and the physical volumes were not independently accessible for direct quotation here.

⚠️ A NOTE ON THIS SOURCE

Three real candidates were considered for this week's planter-diary source: the Aventine Plantation Diary (Adams County), the Locust Grove Cotton Book, and the Everard Green Baker Papers, the strongest of the three because its finding aid explicitly notes that typed transcriptions already exist. None of the three, after a genuine search effort, could be confirmed as freely viewable online without a direct request to the holding archive.

Rather than invent diary language Baker never wrote, this page describes what the collection is known to contain, based on UNC's own cataloging, and points you to the real archive where the actual document is held. This is the same honest standard this course applies whenever a real source cannot be independently verified: describe it accurately, cite it precisely, and do not fabricate its specific words.
