## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **The 1817 Mississippi Constitution's Declaration of Rights opens by establishing that:**
   a) political power is inherent in the people and governments are instituted for their benefit *(correct)*
   b) the governor holds absolute, unchecked authority
   c) only the legislature may interpret the constitution
   d) the federal government appoints all state judges

2. **Article II of the 1817 constitution specifically:**
   a) merges all three branches of government into one office
   b) divides government into legislative, executive, and judicial departments with limited overlap *(correct)*
   c) abolishes the judicial branch entirely
   d) gives the governor sole power to make all state laws

3. **Article VI's provisions on slavery specifically:**
   a) required gradual, compensated emancipation over twenty years
   b) banned bringing any enslaved people into Mississippi from other states
   c) left the entire subject of slavery unaddressed
   d) barred the legislature from freeing enslaved people without owner consent and protected settlers' right to bring enslaved people into the state *(correct)*

4. **Comparing amendment processes, Mississippi's constitution was:**
   a) considerably easier to amend than the U.S. Constitution, requiring only its own legislature and voters *(correct)*
   b) impossible to amend under any circumstances
   c) identical in difficulty to the federal amendment process
   d) amendable only with the U.S. President's personal approval

5. **The U.S. Constitution's language about slavery, compared to Mississippi's constitution, is best described as:**
   a) equally direct and explicit in both documents
   b) more direct than Mississippi's constitution
   c) indirect and euphemistic, reflecting compromise among disagreeing convention delegates *(correct)*
   d) entirely absent from the federal document

6. **The three-fifths clause in the U.S. Constitution specifically addressed:**
   a) how many senators each state could have
   b) how enslaved people counted toward a state's representation in Congress *(correct)*
   c) the international slave trade's permanent prohibition
   d) voting qualifications for federal elections

7. **This week's overall evaluation of Mississippi's political development from Weeks 10 through 14 concludes that:**
   a) the state had solved every first-order political problem completely by 1817
   b) no meaningful government existed in Mississippi before 1817
   c) the state had made genuine progress on several first-order problems while leaving slavery's injustice constitutionally entrenched *(correct)*
   d) slavery was not a significant feature of Mississippi's founding documents

## Primary Source Exercise 14.1 — Mississippi Constitution of 1817

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** The Declaration of Rights states that political power belongs to the people, while Article VI restricts the legislature's power to free enslaved people regardless of later popular will. What tension exists between these two parts of the same document?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why might the convention have wanted to bar the legislature specifically, rather than leave slavery's legal status open to future legislative debate?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Article VI had been written to allow the legislature to end slavery by a simple majority vote at any time. How might Mississippi's subsequent economic and political history have unfolded differently?
4. **(Conceptual)** The constitution's separation-of-powers language (Article II) and its slavery protections (Article VI) both appear in the same document. What does that combination suggest about which of the document's principles the convention treated as most urgent to protect from future change?

## Primary Source Exercise 14.2 — The U.S. Constitution, 1787

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): historical causation, conceptual, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Historical causation)** Why would the delegates at the Constitutional Convention choose euphemistic language like "other Persons" instead of naming slavery directly?
2. **(Conceptual)** The fugitive slave clause requires escaped enslaved people to be "delivered up" regardless of the laws of the state they escaped to. What does this clause reveal about how far individual states' own laws could be overridden by this particular federal requirement?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the delegates had refused to include any of these three slavery-related clauses. How might that decision have affected the Constitution's ratification by the states that did include them?
4. **(Conceptual)** Compare this document's indirect handling of slavery to Mississippi's constitution's direct handling of the same subject in Primary Source 14.1. What does that contrast suggest about the difference between a document produced by broad, difficult compromise and one produced by a narrower, more unified group?

## Discussion

**Prompt:** This week's lessons showed that the U.S. Constitution handles slavery indirectly, through compromise language, while Mississippi's constitution addresses it directly and explicitly. Which approach do you think is more historically honest: a document that names an injustice plainly, or one that obscures it through careful language in order to hold together a larger, more divided group? Use specific details from both constitutions to support your position. Respond to a classmate who reached a different conclusion than you did.

Shared prompt works for both OD and Live sections.
