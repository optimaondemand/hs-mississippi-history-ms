📖 BACKGROUND

The convention that met at Washington, Mississippi, adopted this constitution on August 15, 1817, after a month and eight days of deliberation. The Mississippi Department of Archives and History (MDAH) holds and has digitized the handwritten original, the actual document the convention's delegates signed, as part of its Digital Archives. A typeset English transcription is also available through academic constitutional-law collections for easier reading.

As you read the excerpts below, notice how directly Article VI addresses slavery compared to the more general, principle-based language of the Declaration of Rights that opens the document.

📜 FROM THE CONSTITUTION
"That all political power is inherent in the people, and all free governments are founded on their authority, and instituted for their benefit." (Article I, Declaration of Rights)
"The powers of the government of the state of Mississippi shall be divided into three distinct departments, and each of them confided to a separate body of magistracy, to wit: those which are legislative to one, those which are executive to another, and those which are judicial to another." (Article II)
"The General Assembly shall have no power to pass laws for the emancipation of slaves, without the consent of their owners, unless where a slave shall have rendered to the State some distinguished service… They shall have no power to prevent immigrants to this State from bringing with them such persons as are deemed slaves by the laws of any one of the United States." (Article VI)

Excerpts drawn from the constitution's text as described by the Mississippi Department of Archives and History (MDAH) and the Mississippi Encyclopedia. View the digitized original at MDAH's Digital Archives.
