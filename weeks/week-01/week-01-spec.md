## Primary Source Exercise 1.1 — Columbus's Letter to Luis de Santángel

Exactly 4 questions. Types (never shown to students): counterfactual, historical causation, conceptual, historical causation (order randomized in the actual Canvas item).

1. **(Conceptual)** Santángel had this letter printed and circulated publicly rather than keeping it as a private report to the crown. What does that choice suggest about one of Columbus's purposes in writing it?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why did Columbus describe the islands as "the Indies" rather than naming them as something entirely new?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Santángel had kept the letter private instead of having it printed. Would Spain's interest in funding further voyages have grown as quickly? Explain your reasoning using details from the letter.
4. **(Historical causation)** The excerpt emphasizes that Columbus took possession of the islands "without opposition." Why might Columbus have chosen to highlight that detail in a letter meant to secure future support from the crown?

## Primary Source Exercise 1.2 — The Treaty of Tordesillas

Exactly 4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, counterfactual, historical causation, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** The treaty divides land based on a line "from pole to pole" that neither Spain nor Portugal had actually surveyed. What does this reveal about how European powers of this period understood their right to claim land?
2. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Portugal had not pushed for this treaty at all. Based on what you know about the rivalry between Spain and Portugal, what might have happened instead?
3. **(Historical causation)** Why did Spain and Portugal turn to a treaty, backed by the Pope, rather than settling their competing claims through war?
4. **(Conceptual)** The treaty's line of demarcation assumes that European monarchs had the authority to divide land occupied by people who were never part of the negotiation. What does that assumption tell you about how Spain and Portugal viewed their own authority relative to the peoples they would encounter?

## Discussion (1 per week)

**Prompt:** Columbus's letter was written to persuade the Spanish crown to keep funding his voyages. The Treaty of Tordesillas was written to settle a dispute between two governments. Pick one of the two documents and explain, using specific details from it, how its purpose shaped what it says and how it says it. Then respond to a classmate who picked the other document: what's one thing their document's purpose explains that yours doesn't, or vice versa?

Same prompt works for both OD (asynchronous Canvas discussion board) and Live (can run as a synchronous conversation) per the OD/Live flag in `plan.md` Section 5.

## VR Activity — "Charting the Crossing"

Following the shape in `optima-vr-curriculum`. Platform: ENGAGE.

**Title:** Charting the Crossing

**What you're doing:** You'll trace the outbound route of Columbus's 1492 voyage on a large chart, then compare the two documents from this week's lessons side by side to see how each one's purpose shaped what it says.

**What you'll need:** VR headset (desktop/mobile fallback available), this week's two lesson pages and two primary source pages already read.

**How to join:** [Session type: temporary] · Session ID: [TBD] · Location: [TBD — a map-room or age-of-exploration-themed Location; specific Location name to be confirmed once available in the ENGAGE library] · Spawn point: at the large wall chart.

**What to do, in order:**
1. At the wall chart, use the pre-placed pins (or a Whiteboard IFX, if pins are not available in the chosen Location) to trace Columbus's outbound route: Spain, south to the Canary Islands, then west across the Atlantic to landfall in the Bahamas.
2. Place two sticky notes near the chart: one summarizing Columbus's letter (its purpose: persuading the crown) and one summarizing the Treaty of Tordesillas (its purpose: settling a dispute between governments).
3. Record a short spatial recording (60–90 seconds) explaining, in your own words, how each document's purpose changed what it says.

**What to submit:** Your spatial recording, submitted through Canvas.

**Time:** Approximately 20 minutes.

**Facilitator note:** Students sometimes conflate "what a document says" with "what actually happened." Listen for that conflation in their recordings and, if you're supporting a live section, ask a clarifying question in the moment rather than correcting it after the fact. Be ready to demonstrate placing a sticky note and starting a spatial recording if a student gets stuck on the mechanics.

**Success criteria:** Completion-based. The recording traces the route, names both documents' purposes accurately, and reflects the student's own reasoning rather than reading directly off a lesson page.
