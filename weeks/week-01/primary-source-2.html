The Treaty of Tordesillas, 1494
Primary Source
📖 BACKGROUND

Word of Columbus's voyage spread fast, and Spain and Portugal both wanted to protect their claims to whatever lay across the Atlantic. Portugal had spent decades exploring the coast of Africa and did not want Spain's new discoveries to threaten its own overseas ambitions. To settle the dispute before it turned into a war, representatives of both crowns met at Tordesillas, Spain, in 1494 and agreed on a treaty backed by the Pope.

The treaty drew an imaginary line through the Atlantic Ocean. As you read the excerpt below, think about what it means for two European powers to divide land neither of them had actually seen, without asking anyone who already lived there.

📜 FROM THE TREATY
"A boundary or straight line be determined and drawn north and south, from pole to pole, on the said ocean sea, from the Arctic to the Antarctic pole. This boundary or line shall be drawn straight, as aforesaid, at a distance of three hundred and seventy leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands."

Excerpt from the English translation hosted by the Avalon Project, Yale Law School. Everything discovered east of this line was claimed for Portugal; everything west of it was claimed for Spain.
