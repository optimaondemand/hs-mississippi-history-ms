📖 BACKGROUND

On his voyage home from his first Atlantic crossing, Columbus wrote a letter announcing what he believed he had found. He addressed it to Luis de Santángel, a Spanish court official who had helped convince Queen Isabella to fund the voyage in the first place. Santángel had the letter printed and circulated soon after it arrived in Spain in March 1493, which means this was not a private message. It was written, in part, to be read publicly and to build support for further voyages.

Read the excerpt below carefully. Notice what Columbus chooses to emphasize, and what that choice tells you about his purpose in writing.

📜 FROM THE LETTER
"As I know you will be rejoiced at the glorious success that our Lord has given me in my voyage, I write this to tell you how in thirty-three days I sailed to the Indies with the fleet that the illustrious King and Queen, our Sovereigns, gave me, where I discovered a great many islands, inhabited by numberless people; and of all I have taken possession for their Highnesses by proclamation and display of the Royal Standard without opposition."

Translated excerpt as transcribed by ushistory.org. Columbus called the lands he reached "the Indies" because he believed he had arrived near Asia, not a separate part of the world.
