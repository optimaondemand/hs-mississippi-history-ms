📖 BACKGROUND, AND A NOTE ON THIS SOURCE

This week's second primary source is a runaway advertisement, a real and historically important type of document, but one this course selected only after careful consideration, since it involves describing a real, named, enslaved person as an object of pursuit. The Library of Congress's Chronicling America project preserves this specific advertisement, which ran in the Southern Telegraph (Rodney, Mississippi) beginning around August 9, 1836, with the heading "TWENTY DOLLARS REWARD. RAN AWAY," seeking the return of a man named Peter. The advertisement ran repeatedly from roughly May through September of 1836, and also appeared in the Natchez Courier.

This page describes the advertisement's confirmed, documented details, the newspaper, the dates, the reward amount, and its repeated publication, rather than inventing Peter's physical description or his owner's exact wording, which could not be independently confirmed through the access available in this pass. A Library of Congress research effort into this specific advertisement found no later "in jail" notice reporting Peter's capture, a detail worth holding onto honestly: it does not prove Peter reached lasting freedom, but it leaves real, genuine room for that possibility.
