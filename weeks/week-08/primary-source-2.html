📖 BACKGROUND

After Spain seized the Natchez District from Britain in 1779, Spanish colonial officials began keeping their own court and land records for the district, covering the years 1781 to 1798. These records, written in Spanish, were later translated and preserved by researchers working with the Mississippi Department of Archives and History.

This page describes, rather than quotes directly, what these records generally cover, since a specific translated excerpt was not accessible to confirm word-for-word while this page was built. The confirmed real archive is linked below for further research.

📜 WHAT THE RECORDS COVER

Spanish administration of the Natchez District largely preserved existing British-era land claims rather than starting over from scratch, while also processing new land grant requests from settlers, including many Americans who moved into Spanish territory seeking farmland during and after the American Revolution. Spanish court records from this period document property disputes, land titles, and the daily legal business of a district that, by this point, had already been governed under three different European flags within living memory of many of its residents.

This pattern of accommodation, a new government working with, rather than erasing, the legal claims of the previous one, echoes what you read in Lesson 8.2 about institutions outlasting the governments that created them.
