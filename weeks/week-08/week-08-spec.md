## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **Britain gained the Natchez District in 1763 as a result of:**
   a) the Treaty of Paris, which ended the French and Indian War *(correct)*
   b) a direct purchase from the Choctaw Nation
   c) a naval battle fought in the Gulf of Mexico
   d) a rebellion by French colonists against their own government

2. **British settlement policy in West Florida differed from French colonization mainly by:**
   a) forbidding any new settlers from entering the colony
   b) recruiting settlers from Britain's already-established Atlantic colonies through land grants *(correct)*
   c) relying entirely on a single trading company
   d) requiring all settlers to convert to Catholicism

3. **British control of the Natchez District ended in 1779 when:**
   a) France reconquered the territory
   b) the Chickasaw drove out all British settlers
   c) Spain seized West Florida during the American Revolution *(correct)*
   d) Britain voluntarily abandoned the territory

4. **Spain's approach to Mississippi, as shown by De Soto's expedition, prioritized:**
   a) long-term trade relationships with Native nations
   b) recruiting settlers from other colonies
   c) rapid conquest and the pursuit of existing wealth *(correct)*
   d) building permanent towns and government immediately

5. **Comparing all three colonial powers shows that each one's approach was shaped by:**
   a) pure chance, with no connection to each power's prior experience
   b) instructions from the Catholic Church alone
   c) agreements made jointly between all three powers in advance
   d) that power's own prior colonial experience and immediate priorities *(correct)*

6. **The pattern of Spanish officials preserving British-era land claims in the Natchez District illustrates:**
   a) institutions and legal claims often outlasting the specific government that created them *(correct)*
   b) governments starting entirely from scratch after every political change
   c) Spain's refusal to recognize any prior land claims
   d) a complete breakdown of legal order in the district

7. **This lesson's emphasis on contingency suggests that Mississippi's colonial outcomes were:**
   a) predetermined from the moment each European power arrived
   b) entirely unrelated to events happening in Europe
   c) decided solely by Native nations themselves
   d) the result of specific, changeable circumstances rather than inevitable destiny *(correct)*

## Primary Source Exercise 8.1 — Mississippi Provincial Archives, English Dominion

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** This brief record describes the Natchez post's location by its distance from New Orleans rather than by any local landmark. What does that choice of reference point suggest about whose perspective this document was written from?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why would French forces have already abandoned the post before British officials arrived to take control of it?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose the French had not abandoned the post, and British and French forces had both tried to occupy it simultaneously. How might that have changed the transition described here?
4. **(Conceptual)** This is an official administrative record, not a personal account. What kinds of details about the actual transition, the experience of French, British, and Native residents, might a document like this leave out entirely?

## Primary Source Exercise 8.2 — Spanish-Period Records of the Natchez District

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Why might a new government find it easier to preserve existing land claims from a previous government than to start over with an entirely new system?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why would American settlers specifically be motivated to seek land grants in Spanish territory during and after the American Revolution?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Spain had voided all British-era land claims immediately upon taking control in 1779. How might that decision have affected the district's stability and population?
4. **(Conceptual)** This page describes these records rather than quoting them directly, since a specific translated excerpt could not be confirmed. Why does that distinction, between description and direct quotation, matter when you evaluate a source's reliability?

**Title:** Four Flags Over Natchez

**What you're doing:** You'll examine the same location, the Natchez District, across three different colonial administrations, and reflect on what changed and what stayed the same each time.

**What you'll need:** VR headset (desktop/mobile fallback available), this week's two lessons and two primary sources already read.

**What to do, in order:**
1. Place three sticky notes at the fort, one labeled French, one British, and one Spanish, each noting one specific fact about that power's approach to governing this location.
2. Place a fourth sticky note summarizing one thing that stayed consistent (a land claim, a population, a building) across all three administrations.
3. Record a short spatial recording (60–90 seconds) explaining what this single location's history teaches about the difference between political control and lasting institutions.

**What to submit:** Your spatial recording, submitted through Canvas.

**Success criteria:** Completion-based. The recording accurately distinguishes each power's approach and identifies a genuine element of continuity across all three.
