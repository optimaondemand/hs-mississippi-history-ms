## Review Activity — "A Community and Its Contradiction"

**Format:** Comparative essay and chart, submitted as a document (per `optima-m365-skills` conventions for the eventual Canvas build).

**Instructions to students:** Build a timeline spanning Weeks 10–17, plotting at least eight events in chronological order (for example: Galvez's 1779 capture of Baton Rouge and Fort Panmure, the 1781 Natchez uprising, the 1798 Territory Act, the Treaty of Doak's Stand (1820), the move to Jackson (1821–1822), the Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek (1830), the 1817 Enabling Act and constitution placed in proper sequence relative to the others despite predating several of these events, and the 1857 slave code). For each event, add one sentence explaining why it mattered, not just what happened.

Then write a short comparative essay (250–400 words) answering directly: how did Mississippi's real achievement in building self-government (Weeks 10–14) relate to the economic and social system it built on enslaved labor (Weeks 15–17)? Use specific details and direct quotations from at least three different weeks' primary sources to support the argument.

**Success criteria:** The timeline is in correct chronological order with accurate dates. The essay directly engages the relationship between the quarter's two halves rather than treating them as unrelated topics, and cites specific primary-source evidence rather than only lesson summaries.

**OD/Live note:** Shared assignment for both sections; no divergence needed for this activity.

## Quarter 2 Exam (28 questions, standards 4–5, not cumulative)

Assembled from the weekly reading-quiz and primary-source-exercise banks built in Weeks 10–17, selected and lightly adapted to stand alone in an exam context. Weighted toward standard 4 (statehood and government, Weeks 10–14, which spans five content weeks) with substantial coverage of standard 5 (economy and slavery, Weeks 15–17). Distribution below avoids any repeating answer-position pattern; verify again once final questions are selected for the actual Canvas build.

**Standard 4 — Colonial/Revolutionary Mississippi and Statehood (Weeks 10–14, 17 questions):**
1. Why did Spain declare war on Britain in 1779? *(Week 10)*
2. How did Fort Panmure at Natchez come under Spanish control in 1779? *(Week 10)*
3. What triggered the 1781 Natchez uprising? *(Week 10)*
4. How did Spanish officials generally handle the district's religious and legal diversity? *(Week 10)*
5. What did the Treaty of Doak's Stand's own preamble describe as its purpose? *(Week 11)*
6. What specific change pushed toward full removal between 1820 and 1830? *(Week 11)*
7. What did Article IV of the Treaty of Dancing Rabbit Creek promise? *(Week 11)*
8. What happened to most Choctaw families who tried to use Article XIV? *(Week 11)*
9. What issue did the 1798 Territory Act address before defining the territory itself? *(Week 12)*
10. How did the 1798 act handle slavery compared to the Northwest Ordinance? *(Week 12)*
11. What portion of the original territory did the 1817 Enabling Act authorize as a state? *(Week 12)*
12. What condition did Congress attach to Mississippi's admission regarding the Mississippi River? *(Week 12)*
13. Why did David Holmes's role provide genuine continuity for the new state? *(Week 13)*
14. Why was Mississippi's new capital named Jackson? *(Week 13)*
15. What did Article II of the 1817 constitution establish? *(Week 14)*
16. What did Article VI's slavery provisions specifically protect? *(Week 14)*
17. How does the U.S. Constitution's language about slavery compare to Mississippi's constitution? *(Week 14)*

**Standard 5 — Economy, Slavery, and Antebellum Society (Weeks 15–17, 11 questions):**
18. What specific problem did the cotton gin solve? *(Week 15)*
19. What drove much of Mississippi's population growth in the antebellum period? *(Week 15)*
20. What does a genuine "planter" mean by the common historical definition? *(Week 15)*
21. What drove much of Mississippi's enslaved population growth between 1820 and 1860? *(Week 16)*
22. What did the 1857 slave code require for an enslaved person to travel? *(Week 16)*
23. What do narrators like Liza McGhee and Rhoda Hunt illustrate about WPA narratives? *(Week 16)*
24. What happened to poorer white farmers as planter wealth concentrated, per Olmsted? *(Week 17)*
25. What bound poor whites to a social structure offering them little economic benefit? *(Week 17)*
26. What context matters when reading Olmsted's account of Mississippi? *(Week 17)*
27. What does a runaway advertisement reveal despite being written from the enslaver's perspective? *(Week 17)*
28. What is this quarter's overall evaluation of Mississippi's political and economic development? *(Week 18, review lesson)*
