📖 BACKGROUND

Bernard Romans was a Dutch-born surveyor and naturalist who traveled through British-controlled Florida and the surrounding region in the early 1770s, publishing his observations in 1775. His book includes some of the earliest detailed European descriptions of Choctaw and Chickasaw peoples, alongside accounts of plants, animals, and geography.

As you read, notice that Romans is a European outsider making a judgment about people whose language and customs he did not fully share. Ask yourself what his own assumptions might be adding to, or distorting in, what he actually observed.

📜 FROM THE BOOK
"The Choctaws, who have a greater idea of a meum and tuum than any of their neighbours, are not so hospitable as they; but although they will, on this principle, require pay for even the least morsel they set before one, yet the same idea of property has taken off much of their savage dispositions in many other cases."

Excerpt transcribed from the full text hosted by the Internet Archive. Spelling has been lightly regularized from the original 18th-century printing (the original used the historical "long s" typesetting convention). Meum and tuum is Latin for "mine and thine," a phrase referring to ideas of personal property.
