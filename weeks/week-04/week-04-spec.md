## Reading quiz (7 questions, covers both lessons, inference-based)

1. **Mississippian societies were able to support larger, more permanent settlements than earlier peoples mainly because of:**
   a) improved weapons technology
   b) intensive corn farming in fertile river soil *(correct)*
   c) contact with European traders
   d) a sudden change in climate that ended droughts

2. **The wide geographic spread of shared religious symbols across Mississippian sites (the Southeastern Ceremonial Complex) suggests that:**
   a) all Mississippian peoples spoke the same language
   b) a single empire controlled the entire region
   c) distant Mississippian communities shared some common religious beliefs despite political differences *(correct)*
   d) Mississippian religion was borrowed entirely from Europe

3. **The Natchez were especially useful to historians studying Mississippian culture because:**
   a) they left behind written records of their own
   b) they were the only Mississippian society that ever existed
   c) archaeologists have found more Natchez sites than any other kind
   d) they maintained a mound-centered, chief-led society into the 1700s, when Europeans could observe it directly *(correct)*

4. **Choctaw political authority was distributed across many towns and leaders mainly because:**
   a) the nation's large territory and many farming communities suited decision-making by discussion and consensus *(correct)*
   b) Choctaw religion forbade a single central ruler
   c) European colonists required it
   d) the Choctaw had no concept of leadership at all

5. **In the Choctaw clan system, a person's identity and social relationships traced primarily through:**
   a) their father's line
   b) their town's founding date
   c) their personal wealth
   d) their mother's line *(correct)*

6. **The Chickasaw earned a reputation among European powers mainly for:**
   a) large-scale farming exports
   b) building the tallest mounds in the region
   c) military strength and skilled, difficult-to-defeat negotiation *(correct)*
   d) refusing all contact with Europeans

7. **The Great Sun's authority among the Natchez was unusual compared to Choctaw or Chickasaw leadership because it was:**
   a) elected by popular vote each year
   b) centralized and carried strong religious as well as political weight *(correct)*
   c) shared equally among several towns
   d) limited entirely to military matters

## Primary Source Exercise 4.1 — Le Page du Pratz, *The History of Louisiana*

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, historical causation, counterfactual, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Why does the book's opening emphasize du Pratz's sixteen years of experience and his role as an administrator, rather than simply presenting his observations directly?
2. **(Historical causation)** Why would living among the Natchez for an extended period, rather than visiting briefly, make du Pratz's account more valuable to later historians?
3. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose du Pratz had visited Louisiana for only a few weeks instead of living there for sixteen years. How might that change how much historians today trust his account?
4. **(Conceptual)** What is the difference between a source being detailed and a source being accurate? Does establishing du Pratz's credentials address both, one, or neither of these questions?

---

## Primary Source Exercise 4.2 — Bernard Romans, *A Concise Natural History of East and West Florida*

4 questions. Types (never shown to students): conceptual, counterfactual, historical causation, conceptual.

1. **(Conceptual)** Romans describes Choctaw ideas about property as having "taken off much of their savage dispositions." What does this phrasing reveal about Romans's own assumptions, separate from whatever he actually observed?
2. **(Counterfactual)** Suppose Romans had grown up within Choctaw society himself instead of observing it as an outside surveyor. How might his description of the same customs have been different?
3. **(Historical causation)** Why might a European observer in the 1770s specifically notice and comment on a Native nation's ideas about property and payment?
4. **(Conceptual)** Can a source be useful evidence about a real historical practice (ideas of property among the Choctaw) even while also reflecting the author's own bias? Explain your reasoning.

## Discussion

**Prompt:** This week's two primary sources are both written by European men describing Native nations from the outside. What can historians learn from sources like these, and what can they never fully learn from them? Use specific details from both sources to support your answer. Respond to a classmate who identified a different limitation than you did.
