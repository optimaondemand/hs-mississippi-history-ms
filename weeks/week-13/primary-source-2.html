📖 BACKGROUND

The Southern Galaxy was published in Natchez from June 1828 to March 1830 by W. C. Grissam and Company, overlapping directly with the presidential and gubernatorial elections of 1828 and 1829 described in Lesson 13.2, and running alongside its rival, the Statesman and Gazette, described in Primary Source 13.1. The Library of Congress's Chronicling America project catalogs this title as well (LCCN sn84020041), confirming it as a real, dated Natchez newspaper of this period, competing directly with the Statesman and Gazette for the same readership.

⚠️ A NOTE ON THIS SOURCE

As with Primary Source 13.1, this paper's existence, exact publication dates, publisher, and cataloging are confirmed through the Library of Congress's own records. A specific issue's text could not be independently confirmed here: repeated attempts to retrieve readable page images or searchable text from the Library of Congress's servers did not succeed through the access available in this pass. Rather than fabricate a headline or quotation this paper never printed, this page describes it honestly as a real, cataloged source whose day-to-day contents are not verified here.

Two rival newspapers publishing in the same small city during the same contested elections, as the Southern Galaxy and the Statesman and Gazette did, is itself meaningful evidence about Natchez's political culture in this period, even without a specific quoted excerpt from either. A single town supporting two competing partisan papers at once suggests a genuinely engaged, actively divided readership, exactly the kind of political competition Lesson 13.2 described.
