📖 BACKGROUND

This week's primary sources depart from most of the course's earlier weeks. Governor David Holmes's own papers, the most obvious first-person source for Mississippi's early state government, are confirmed non-digitized and not available online. In their place, this week draws on two competing Natchez newspapers from the 1820s that covered the very elections and political rivalries Lesson 13.2 described.

The Statesman and Gazette was published in Natchez from May 1825 to October 1832, formed from the merger of two earlier Natchez papers, the Mississippi Statesman and the Natchez Gazette. Its publication run spans the very presidential and gubernatorial contests of 1828 and 1829 described in Lesson 13.2. The Library of Congress's Chronicling America project catalogs this title (LCCN sn83016906) as part of its national historic newspaper collection.

⚠️ A NOTE ON THIS SOURCE

This paper's real existence, publication dates, and cataloging are confirmed through the Library of Congress's own records. What could not be confirmed in this pass is the specific text of any single issue: the Library of Congress's Chronicling America servers did not return readable page images or searchable text through the access available here, after repeated attempts. Rather than invent a quotation or headline this paper never printed, this page describes the newspaper honestly, as a real, cataloged, and dated historical source whose specific contents on any given day are not independently verified here.

As a competing Natchez paper of this period, the Statesman and Gazette would have covered local and national political contests, printed correspondence and editorials reflecting its own political leanings, and carried advertisements, shipping notices, and legal announcements typical of an early American commercial newspaper, the same general mix of content described for its rival, the Southern Galaxy, in Primary Source 13.2.
